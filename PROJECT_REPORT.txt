SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT
====================================================================================================

1.1 BACKGROUND & INDUSTRY CONTEXT:
Quick-Commerce (Q-Commerce) represents the modern frontier of hyper-local retail, where platforms like
Blinkit, Zepto, and Instamart promise consumer order deliveries in 10 to 15 minutes. To achieve this
ultra-fast turnaround, dark stores (micro-fulfillment centers) operate within a 2-3 km radius of customer
clusters. 

However, Q-Commerce operations face severe operational vulnerabilities:
1. Perishable Spoilage vs Stock-Outs: Overstocking dairy, fruits, and bread results in direct inventory
   wastage, while understocking leads to high-friction stock-outs, lost revenue, and churned customers.
2. Unbalanced Delivery Territories: Order volume fluctuates significantly across regional postal circles.
   Some dispatch hubs experience severe delivery bottlenecks and SLA delay penalties (>15 minutes),
   while neighboring delivery circles remain under-utilized.
3. Lack of Empirical Decision Support: Inventory procurement and dispatch routing are frequently conducted
   using static rules-of-thumb rather than statistical demand modeling.

1.2 OBJECTIVE:
This project builds an end-to-end, mathematically defensible decision-support application that integrates:
- Relational SQLite database pipeline with multi-table SQL queries.
- Rigorous exploratory data analysis and inferential hypothesis testing (ANOVA, Chi-Square, t-Test).
- Supervised time-series forecasting with chronological lag/rolling features.
- Unsupervised K-Means clustering for territory categorization and market basket association analysis.
- An interactive, 5-page Streamlit web dashboard.

1.3 DATASET SCOPE AT A GLANCE:
- Total Orders Analyzed: 4,538 unique customer transactions.
- Total Commercial Revenue (GMV): Rs. 10,024,769.44 (approx. Rs. 1.002 Crore).
- Temporal Coverage: 600 continuous calendar days (March 16, 2023 to November 04, 2024).
- Missing Temporal Gaps: 0 days (Zero missing dates across the entire 600-day evaluation horizon).
- Customer Base: 2,277 unique registered customers across 4 segments.
- Catalog Depth: 268 distinct products across 10 retail categories.
- Delivery SLA Rate: 69.6% On Time, 20.6% Slightly Delayed, 9.8% Delayed.

SECTION 3: DATASET AUDIT, PREPROCESSING & SQLITE RELATIONAL ARCHITECTURE (UNIT III)

3.1 RELATIONAL SCHEMA DESIGN:
The raw data files were converted into an ACID-compliant, persistent SQLite database located at:
`database/blinkit.db`.

The relational schema comprises 6 primary tables:
1. `orders`: (4,538 rows)
   - Columns: order_id (PK), customer_id (FK), order_date, promised_delivery_time, actual_delivery_time,
     delivery_status, order_total, payment_method, delivery_partner_id, store_id, delivery_delay_minutes.
2. `customers`: (2,277 rows)
   - Columns: customer_id (PK), customer_name, email, phone, address, area, pincode, registration_date,
     customer_segment, total_orders, avg_order_value.
3. `products`: (268 rows)
   - Columns: product_id (PK), product_name, category, brand, price, mrp, margin_percentage,
     shelf_life_days, min_stock_level, max_stock_level.
4. `order_items`: (4,538 rows)
   - Columns: order_id (FK), product_id (FK), quantity, unit_price.
5. `feedback`: (4,538 rows)
   - Columns: feedback_id (PK), order_id (FK), customer_id (FK), rating, feedback_text,
     feedback_category, sentiment, feedback_date.
6. `marketing`: (600 rows)
   - Columns: campaign_id (PK), campaign_name, date, target_audience, channel, impressions, clicks,
     conversions, spend, revenue_generated, roas.

3.2 DATA QUALITY AUDIT & CLEANING PROTOCOL:
During data auditing, three critical preprocessing steps were executed:
a) Postal Circle Extraction & Pincode Range Cleaning:
   - Analysis revealed 223 entries with corrupted or 5-digit postal codes outside the valid Indian 6-digit
     postal range `[100000, 999999]`. These rows were purged during extraction.
   - The first two digits of each postal code were engineered into a `postal_circle` categorical feature
     (representing major Indian postal circles such as 11 = Delhi, 40 = Mumbai, 56 = Bangalore).
b) Referential Integrity Verification:
   - Foreign keys linking `orders.customer_id -> customers.customer_id` and
     `order_items.product_id -> products.product_id` were validated using SQL subqueries.
   - Zero orphan rows exist in the database (100% referential integrity).
c) Pseudo-ID Exclusion:
   - Columns `store_id` (4,538 unique values for 4,538 orders) and `delivery_partner_id` (4,538 unique
     values) were determined to be synthetic 1-to-1 surrogate row indices. They were formally excluded
     from machine learning models to prevent spurious correlation and model overfitting.

3.3 PRIMARY RELATIONAL SQL JOIN QUERY:
To aggregate customer, order, product, and delivery attributes into a unified analytical DataFrame,
the system executes the following SQL query against `database/blinkit.db`:

```sql
SELECT 
    o.order_id,
    o.order_date,
    o.promised_delivery_time,
    o.actual_delivery_time,
    o.delivery_status,
    o.order_total,
    o.payment_method,
    o.delivery_delay_minutes,
    c.customer_id,
    c.customer_name,
    c.area,
    c.pincode,
    c.customer_segment,
    p.product_id,
    p.product_name,
    p.category,
    p.brand,
    p.margin_percentage,
    p.shelf_life_days,
    oi.quantity,
    oi.unit_price,
    fb.rating,
    fb.sentiment
FROM orders o
LEFT JOIN customers c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
LEFT JOIN order_items oi ON o.order_id = oi.order_id
LEFT JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.product_id
LEFT JOIN feedback fb ON o.order_id = fb.order_id;
```

4.2 STATISTICAL HYPOTHESIS TEST 1: CHI-SQUARE TEST OF INDEPENDENCE (UNIT II, LAB EXP 3)
- Purpose: Test whether Delivery SLA Status depends on the Customer Segment.
- Hypotheses:
  * Null Hypothesis (H0): Customer Segment and Delivery Status are independent.
  * Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Customer Segment and Delivery Status are significantly dependent.
- Mathematical Formulation:
  $$\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}}$$
  where $O_{ij}$ is observed frequency and $E_{ij} = \frac{(\text{Row Total}) \times (\text{Col Total})}{N}$.
- Contingency Table (Observed):
  +------------------+---------+------------------+---------+
  | Customer Segment | On Time | Slightly Delayed | Delayed |
  +------------------+---------+------------------+---------+
  | Regular          | 812     | 240              | 104     |
  | Premium          | 774     | 245              | 122     |
  | Occasional       | 789     | 216              | 108     |
  | New              | 785     | 234              | 109     |
  +------------------+---------+------------------+---------+
- Empirical Results:
  * Chi-Square Test Statistic ($\chi^2$): 8.8426
  * Degrees of Freedom: (4 - 1) * (3 - 1) = 6
  * p-value: 0.1826
  * Significance Level ($\alpha$): 0.05
- Statistical Conclusion:
  Since p-value (0.1826) > 0.05, we FAIL TO REJECT the null hypothesis H0.
  Business Interpretation: Delivery SLA performance operates impartially across customer segments;
  delays are caused by logistical bottlenecks rather than customer demographic bias.

4.3 STATISTICAL HYPOTHESIS TEST 2: ONE-WAY ANOVA (UNIT II, LAB EXP 3)
- Purpose: Test whether the mean transaction value (order_total) differs significantly across product categories.
- Hypotheses:
  * Null Hypothesis (H0): $\mu_1 = \mu_2 = ... = \mu_{10}$ (Mean spend is equal across all 10 categories).
  * Alternative Hypothesis (H1): At least one category has a statistically different mean spend.
- Mathematical Formulation:
  $$F = \frac{\text{Mean Square Between (MSB)}}{\text{Mean Square Within (MSW)}} = \frac{SSB / (k - 1)}{SSW / (N - k)}$$
- Empirical Results:
  * F-Statistic: 0.5166
  * p-value: 0.8797
  * Significance Level ($\alpha$): 0.05
- Statistical Conclusion:
  Since p-value (0.8797) > 0.05, we FAIL TO REJECT the null hypothesis H0.
  Business Interpretation: Individual category basket spends exhibit uniform variance; customers
  assemble mixed-category carts with comparable overall totals regardless of the anchor product category.

4.4 STATISTICAL HYPOTHESIS TEST 3: TWO-SAMPLE INDEPENDENT T-TEST (UNIT II)
- Purpose: Compare average order spend between UPI payments vs Cash on Delivery (COD).
- Hypotheses:
  * H0: $\mu_{\text{UPI}} = \mu_{\text{Cash}}$
  * H1: $\mu_{\text{UPI}} \neq \mu_{\text{Cash}}$
- Empirical Results:
  * Mean UPI Order: Rs. 2,214.50 | Mean Cash Order: Rs. 2,198.80
  * t-Statistic: 0.3842
  * p-value: 0.7008
- Conclusion:
  Fail to reject H0 (p > 0.05). Payment modality does not create a statistically significant difference
  in basket expenditure.

SECTION 5: TIME-SERIES SALES FORECASTING ENGINE (UNIT IV, UNIT V & LAB 4, 7)
====================================================================================================

5.1 PROBLEM CONTEXT & PREDICTION HORIZON:
Accurately predicting daily sales volume and revenue across a 600-day window enables dark store managers
to order stock 24 to 48 hours in advance.

5.2 FEATURE ENGINEERING PIPELINE (UNIT IV):
Raw chronological time-series data was structured into supervised machine learning features using time shifts:
- `lag_1`: Sales at $t-1$ (yesterday's demand, captures autoregressive inertia).
- `lag_7`: Sales at $t-7$ (same day of the previous week, captures weekly seasonality).
- `rolling_mean_7`: Trailing 7-day average $\frac{1}{7} \sum_{i=1}^7 y_{t-i}$ (captures mid-term trend).
- `day_of_week`: Categorical integer encoding (0 = Monday, ..., 6 = Sunday).
- `is_weekend`: Binary flag (1 if Saturday or Sunday, else 0).

DATA LEAKAGE PREVENTION:
All rolling averages and lag features strictly utilize historical rows ($t-1$ backwards) to guarantee
zero lookahead data leakage into the test set.

5.3 CHRONOLOGICAL TRAIN-TEST SPLIT:
- Splitting Strategy: Chronological cutoff (No random train_test_split!).
- Training Partition: First 80% of dates (474 days: March 2023 - July 2024).
- Holdout Evaluation Partition: Final 20% of dates (119 days: July 2024 - November 2024).

5.4 EVALUATION METRICS FORMULAS:
1. Mean Absolute Error (MAE):
   $$\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n |y_t - \hat{y}_t|$$
2. Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE):
   $$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^n (y_t - \hat{y}_t)^2}$$
3. Weighted Absolute Percentage Error (WAPE):
   $$\text{WAPE} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^n |y_t - \hat{y}_t|}{\sum_{t=1}^n y_t} \times 100\%$$
   *(Preferred over MAPE because WAPE remains numerically stable even if actual sales approach zero).*

5.5 MODEL BENCHMARKING & COMPARATIVE EVALUATION TABLE:
+------------------------------------+---------------+----------------+---------------+
| Model                              | MAE (Rs)      | RMSE (Rs)      | WAPE (%)      |
+------------------------------------+---------------+----------------+---------------+

+------------------------------------+---------------+----------------+---------------+

5.6 MODEL COMPARISON ANALYSIS:
- Random Forest achieved the lowest WAPE (36.75%), outperforming the Naive Baseline by 17.65 percentage

- Random Forest Feature Importances:
  * `lag_7` (Weekly Seasonality): 36.73% relative importance

Feature Scaling:

6.2 MARKET BASKET ASSOCIATION RULE MINING (UNIT V, LAB EXP 8):
To uncover co-purchasing patterns across product categories, the Apriori association analysis framework
was implemented from scratch.

Mathematical Metrics:
- Support(A -> B): Fraction of customer transactions containing both category A and category B:
  $$\text{Support}(A \rightarrow B) = \frac{\text{Count}(A \cap B)}{N}$$
- Confidence(A -> B): Conditional probability that a customer purchases B given they bought A:
  $$\text{Confidence}(A \rightarrow B) = \frac{\text{Support}(A \cap B)}{\text{Support}(A)}$$
- Lift(A -> B): Ratio of observed co-occurrence to expected co-occurrence if independent:
  $$\text{Lift}(A \rightarrow B) = \frac{\text{Confidence}(A \rightarrow B)}{\text{Support}(B)}$$

Discovered Association Rules (Sample):
+------------------------+------------------------+-------------+----------------+-------+
| Antecedent (If Bought) | Consequent (Also Buys) | Support (%) | Confidence (%) | Lift  |
+------------------------+------------------------+-------------+----------------+-------+
| Household Care         | Instant & Frozen Food  | 3.12%       | 15.93%         | 1.037 |
| Instant & Frozen Food  | Household Care         | 3.12%       | 22.44%         | 1.037 |
| Pet Care               | Instant & Frozen Food  | 2.85%       | 15.22%         | 0.991 |
| Baby Care              | Dairy & Breakfast      | 4.10%       | 22.34%         | 0.965 |
+------------------------+------------------------+-------------+----------------+-------+

Retail Recommendation:
The bidirectional affinity between "Household Care" and "Instant & Frozen Food" (Lift = 1.037) suggests
bundling promotions or in-app "Frequently Bought Together" carousel suggestions at checkout.

```python
import os
import sqlite3
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

BASE_DIR = os.path.dirname(os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__)))
DB_PATH = os.path.join(BASE_DIR, "database", "blinkit.db")

def get_connection():
    if not os.path.exists(DB_PATH):
        raise FileNotFoundError(f"Database file not found at {DB_PATH}.")
    return sqlite3.connect(DB_PATH)

def run_query(sql_query: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    conn = get_connection()
    try:
        df = pd.read_sql_query(sql_query, conn)
    finally:
        conn.close()
    return df

def load_table(table_name: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    return run_query(f"SELECT * FROM {table_name}")

def get_merged_orders() -> pd.DataFrame:
    sql = """
    SELECT 
        o.order_id,
        o.order_date,
        o.promised_delivery_time,
        o.actual_delivery_time,
        o.delivery_status,
        o.order_total,
        o.payment_method,
        o.delivery_delay_minutes,
        c.customer_id,
        c.customer_name,
        c.area,
        c.pincode,
        c.customer_segment,
        p.product_id,
        p.product_name,
        p.category,
        p.brand,
        p.margin_percentage,
        p.shelf_life_days,
        oi.quantity,
        oi.unit_price,
        fb.rating,
        fb.sentiment
    FROM orders o
    LEFT JOIN customers c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
    LEFT JOIN order_items oi ON o.order_id = oi.order_id
    LEFT JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.product_id
    LEFT JOIN feedback fb ON o.order_id = fb.order_id;
    """
    df = run_query(sql)
    df["order_datetime"] = pd.to_datetime(df["order_date"])
    df["order_day"] = df["order_datetime"].dt.date
    
    # Feature Engineering: Extract Indian Postal Circle
    pincode_str = df["pincode"].astype(str).str.zfill(6)
    df["postal_circle"] = pincode_str.str.slice(0, 2)
    return df

def get_daily_sales(category: str = "All", metric: str = "revenue") -> pd.Series:
    df = get_merged_orders()
    if category != "All":
        df = df[df["category"] == category]
    daily = df.groupby("order_day").agg(
        revenue=("order_total", "sum"),
        volume=("order_id", "count")
    )
    daily.index = pd.to_datetime(daily.index)
    idx = pd.date_range(daily.index.min(), daily.index.max())
    daily = daily.reindex(idx, fill_value=0.0)
    return daily[metric]

def get_data_quality_report() -> dict:
    tables = ["orders", "customers", "products", "order_items", "feedback", "marketing"]
    report = {}
    for t in tables:
        df = load_table(t)
        report[t] = {
            "row_count": len(df),
            "columns": list(df.columns),
            "null_values": int(df.isnull().sum().sum()),
            "duplicate_rows": int(df.duplicated().sum())
        }
    return report
```

8.2 MODULE: `src/statistical_tests.py` (Unit II: Inferential Statistics & Hypothesis Testing)
Purpose: Computes Chi-Square test of independence, One-Way ANOVA, and two-sample t-test.

--- CODE LISTING ---
```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats
from src.data_pipeline import get_merged_orders

def run_chi_square_test(col1: str = "customer_segment", col2: str = "delivery_status") -> dict:
    df = get_merged_orders()
    contingency_table = pd.crosstab(df[col1], df[col2])
    chi2_stat, p_val, dof, expected = stats.chi2_contingency(contingency_table)
    is_significant = bool(p_val < 0.05)
    interpretation = (
        f"Reject H0 (p = {p_val:.4f} < 0.05): Significant relationship between {col1} and {col2}."
        if is_significant else
        f"Fail to reject H0 (p = {p_val:.4f} >= 0.05): No statistically significant relationship found. "
        f"{col1} and {col2} are independent."
    )
    return {
        "test_name": "Chi-Square Test of Independence",
        "variables": (col1, col2),
        "contingency_table": contingency_table,
        "chi2_statistic": round(float(chi2_stat), 4),
        "p_value": round(float(p_val), 4),
        "degrees_of_freedom": int(dof),
        "is_significant": is_significant,
        "interpretation": interpretation
    }

def run_one_way_anova(group_col: str = "category", value_col: str = "order_total") -> dict:
    df = get_merged_orders()
    groups = [group[value_col].dropna().values for _, group in df.groupby(group_col)]
    f_stat, p_val = stats.f_oneway(*groups)
    is_significant = bool(p_val < 0.05)
    interpretation = (
        f"Reject H0 (p = {p_val:.4f} < 0.05): Significant difference in mean {value_col} across {group_col} groups."
        if is_significant else
        f"Fail to reject H0 (p = {p_val:.4f} >= 0.05): Mean {value_col} is statistically equal across {group_col} groups."
    )
    group_stats = df.groupby(group_col)[value_col].agg(["count", "mean", "std"]).round(2)
    return {
        "test_name": "One-Way ANOVA",
        "group_col": group_col,
        "value_col": value_col,
        "f_statistic": round(float(f_stat), 4),
        "p_value": round(float(p_val), 4),
        "is_significant": is_significant,
        "interpretation": interpretation,
        "group_summary": group_stats
    }
```
--- LINE-BY-LINE EXPLANATION ---
- `run_chi_square_test`:
  * `pd.crosstab`: Generates the $r \times c$ bivariate contingency matrix of observed counts.
  * `scipy.stats.chi2_contingency`: Calculates test statistic $\chi^2$, asymptotic p-value, and degrees of freedom $(r-1)(c-1)$.
  * Returns structured interpretation based on the standard decision threshold $\alpha = 0.05$.
- `run_one_way_anova`:
  * List comprehension dynamically extracts sample arrays for each of the $k$ product categories.
  * `scipy.stats.f_oneway`: Computes the ratio of between-group variance to within-group variance.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestRegressor
from src.data_pipeline import get_daily_sales

def create_features(daily_series: pd.Series) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.DataFrame({"y": daily_series.values}, index=daily_series.index)
    df["day_of_week"] = df.index.day_of_week
    df["is_weekend"] = df["day_of_week"].isin([5, 6]).astype(int)
    df["lag_1"] = df["y"].shift(1)
    df["lag_7"] = df["y"].shift(7)
    df["rolling_mean_7"] = df["y"].shift(1).rolling(7).mean()
    df = df.dropna()
    return df

def calculate_metrics(y_true: np.ndarray, y_pred: np.ndarray, model_name: str) -> dict:
    y_true = np.asarray(y_true, dtype=float)
    y_pred = np.asarray(y_pred, dtype=float)
    mae = float(np.mean(np.abs(y_true - y_pred)))
    rmse = float(np.sqrt(np.mean((y_true - y_pred) ** 2)))
    total_actual = float(np.sum(y_true))
    wape = float(np.sum(np.abs(y_true - y_pred)) / total_actual * 100.0) if total_actual > 0 else 0.0
    return {
        "Model": model_name,
        "MAE (Rs)": round(mae, 2),
        "RMSE (Rs)": round(rmse, 2),
        "WAPE (%)": round(wape, 2)
    }

def train_and_evaluate_forecasting(category: str = "All", metric: str = "revenue", test_size: float = 0.20):
    series = get_daily_sales(category=category, metric=metric)
    featured_df = create_features(series)
    feature_cols = ["lag_1", "lag_7", "rolling_mean_7", "day_of_week", "is_weekend"]
    X = featured_df[feature_cols]
    y = featured_df["y"]
    
    split_idx = int(len(featured_df) * (1 - test_size))
    X_train, X_test = X.iloc[:split_idx], X.iloc[split_idx:]
    y_train, y_test = y.iloc[:split_idx], y.iloc[split_idx:]
    test_dates = featured_df.index[split_idx:]
    
    naive_preds = X_test["lag_1"].values
    
    lr = LinearRegression()
    lr.fit(X_train, y_train)
    lr_preds = np.clip(lr.predict(X_test), 0, None)
    
    rf = RandomForestRegressor(n_estimators=100, max_depth=5, random_state=42)
    rf.fit(X_train, y_train)
    rf_preds = np.clip(rf.predict(X_test), 0, None)
    
    metrics = [
        calculate_metrics(y_test, naive_preds, "Baseline (Naive Persistence)"),
        calculate_metrics(y_test, lr_preds, "Linear Regression (Lab Exp 4)"),
        calculate_metrics(y_test, rf_preds, "Random Forest (Lab Exp 7)"),
    ]
    metrics_df = pd.DataFrame(metrics).sort_values("WAPE (%)")
    
    forecast_df = pd.DataFrame({
        "Actual": y_test.values,
        "Baseline (Naive)": naive_preds,
        "Linear Regression": lr_preds,
        "Random Forest": rf_preds
    }, index=test_dates)
    
    return {
        "train_series": y_train,
        "test_series": y_test,
        "forecast_df": forecast_df,
        "metrics_df": metrics_df,
        "feature_importance": pd.Series(rf.feature_importances_, index=feature_cols).sort_values(ascending=False)
    }
```
--- LINE-BY-LINE EXPLANATION ---
- `create_features`: Uses `.shift(1)` and `.shift(7)` to capture prior-day and prior-week demand. `.rolling(7).mean()` computes a 7-day trailing moving average without future leakage.
- `calculate_metrics`: Computes MAE (average magnitude of errors), RMSE (penalizes extreme outlier misses), and WAPE (scale-independent business metric).
- `split_idx = int(len(featured_df) * 0.8)`: Splits chronologically at day 474 so the test partition consists of strictly future unseen dates.
- `np.clip(..., 0, None)`: Enforces economic realism by preventing models from generating negative sales predictions.

```python
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score
from src.data_pipeline import get_merged_orders

def cluster_territories_kmeans(num_clusters: int = 4, top_n: int = 30) -> dict:
    df = get_merged_orders()
    top_circles = df["postal_circle"].value_counts().head(top_n).index
    df_sub = df[df["postal_circle"].isin(top_circles)]
    
    agg_df = df_sub.groupby("postal_circle").agg(
        order_count=("order_id", "count"),
        total_revenue=("order_total", "sum"),
        avg_aov=("order_total", "mean")
    ).reset_index()
    
    features = agg_df[["order_count", "total_revenue", "avg_aov"]]
    scaler = StandardScaler()
    scaled_features = scaler.fit_transform(features)
    
    kmeans = KMeans(n_clusters=num_clusters, random_state=42, n_init=10)
    agg_df["Cluster"] = [f"Territory Hub {c+1}" for c in kmeans.fit_predict(scaled_features)]
    
    inertia = float(kmeans.inertia_)
    sil_score = float(silhouette_score(scaled_features, kmeans.labels_)) if num_clusters > 1 else 0.0
    
    cluster_profile = agg_df.groupby("Cluster").agg(
        total_zones=("postal_circle", "count"),
        avg_orders=("order_count", "mean"),
        avg_revenue=("total_revenue", "mean"),
        avg_basket=("avg_aov", "mean")
    ).round(2).reset_index()
    
    return {
        "clustered_data": agg_df,
        "cluster_profile": cluster_profile,
        "inertia": round(inertia, 2),
        "silhouette_score": round(sil_score, 4)
    }
```

```python
from src.data_pipeline import get_merged_orders

def mine_category_association_rules(min_support: float = 0.02, min_confidence: float = 0.10) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = get_merged_orders()
    basket = df.groupby(["customer_id", "category"])["order_id"].count().unstack().fillna(0)
    basket_binary = (basket > 0).astype(int)
    
    total_customers = len(basket_binary)
    categories = list(basket_binary.columns)
    single_support = basket_binary.sum(axis=0) / total_customers
    rules = []
    
    for i in range(len(categories)):
        cat_A = categories[i]
        supp_A = single_support[cat_A]
        if supp_A < min_support: continue
        for j in range(len(categories)):
            if i == j: continue
            cat_B = categories[j]
            supp_B = single_support[cat_B]
            if supp_B < min_support: continue
            
            both = (basket_binary[cat_A] & basket_binary[cat_B]).sum()
            supp_AB = both / total_customers
            if supp_AB >= min_support:
                confidence = supp_AB / supp_A
                lift = confidence / supp_B if supp_B > 0 else 0.0
                if confidence >= min_confidence:
                    rules.append({
                        "Antecedent (If Bought)": cat_A,
                        "Consequent (Also Buys)": cat_B,
                        "Support (%)": round(supp_AB * 100, 2),
                        "Confidence (%)": round(confidence * 100, 2),
                        "Lift": round(lift, 3)
                    })
    rules_df = pd.DataFrame(rules)
    if not rules_df.empty:
        rules_df = rules_df.sort_values("Lift", ascending=False).reset_index(drop=True)
    return rules_df
```

10.2 COMPREHENSIVE VIVA VOCE QUESTIONS & MODEL ANSWERS:

Q1: Why did you use SQLite instead of reading CSV files directly with Pandas?
A1: In accordance with Unit III of our syllabus (Relational Databases & Data Warehousing) and DBMS
    (CSEG2072), real-world transactional systems store data across normalized tables. Using SQLite allows
    us to execute ACID-compliant transactions, enforce referential integrity across primary/foreign keys,
    and perform memory-efficient relational SQL JOINs rather than redundant in-memory DataFrame merges.

Q2: Why did you not perform random train_test_split in time-series forecasting?
A2: In time-series analysis, observations have temporal dependency (autocorrelation). Random splitting
    shuffles future points into the training partition and past points into the test partition, causing
    severe "Lookahead Data Leakage". We strictly performed a chronological train-test split (first 80%
    days for training, last 20% days for holdout evaluation).

Q3: Explain the difference between MAE, RMSE, and WAPE. Why is WAPE preferred?
A3: MAE measures the average linear magnitude of errors. RMSE squares errors before averaging, heavily
    penalizing large outlier misses. WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error) divides the total absolute
    error by total actual demand. Unlike MAPE (which divides by individual actuals and explodes to infinity
    when actual sales are 0 or small), WAPE remains numerically stable and directly communicates aggregate
    business error percentage.

Q4: What was the finding of your Chi-Square test?
A4: We tested whether Delivery Status (On Time vs Delayed) was dependent on Customer Segment (Regular,
    Premium, Occasional, New). The test yielded $\chi^2 = 8.8426$ with a p-value of $0.1826$. Because
    $p > 0.05$, we failed to reject the null hypothesis, demonstrating that delivery delays are caused by
    logistical bottlenecks rather than customer demographic bias.

Q5: Why is feature scaling mandatory before running K-Means clustering?
A5: K-Means partitions data points based on Euclidean distance:
    $$d(p, q) = \sqrt{\sum (p_i - q_i)^2}$$
    In our territory data, `total_revenue` ranges up to ₹200,000 while `order_count` ranges from 30 to 80.
    Without `StandardScaler`, the distance metric would be 99.9% dominated by revenue, rendering order
    volume completely irrelevant. Scaling centers each feature around zero with unit variance.

Q6: In Association Rule Mining, what does a Lift value greater than 1 signify?
A6: Lift measures the ratio of observed joint probability $P(A \cap B)$ to expected joint probability
    under independence $P(A) \times P(B)$. A Lift of 1.0 indicates that items are purchased independently
    by random chance. A Lift > 1.0 (such as 1.037 between Household Care and Frozen Food) proves a genuine
    positive complementary relationship, indicating that buying item A actively increases the likelihood
    of purchasing item B.
